# The study, checked against the paper (November 15, 2026)

Bursztyn, Handel, Jiménez-Durán and Roth, "When Product Markets Become Collective Traps: The Case of Social Media." NBER Working Paper 31771, October 2023, revised July 2024; published in the American Economic Review 115(12), December 2025, pp. 4105–36.

Read from the revised July 2024 paper itself (introduction, pp. 2–3), and matched by the authors' own Brookings article of June 2, 2026:

- More than 1,000 students from various U.S. colleges, in pre-registered, incentivized online experiments.
- Paid to deactivate for four weeks while others keep using it (average): TikTok $55, Instagram $47.
- Willing to PAY to have everyone at their university deactivate, themselves included (average): TikTok $24, Instagram $6.
- Active users who experience negative welfare from the product's existence: TikTok 60%, Instagram 46%.
- With the network removed, the payment needed drops to $39 (TikTok) and $37 (Instagram).
- "product market traps": "some users would prefer the product not to exist, yet they find it optimal to consume it."

Do not use $59, $28 or 64%: those are from the first (October 2023) draft, as repeated in the University of Chicago's research summary, and were superseded.
